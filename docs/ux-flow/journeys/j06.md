Persona: pharmacist (Anil Deshmukh). Goal: a customer with no prescription and no patient record buys 10 Cetirizine 10mg Tablets and 1 Sunscreen SPF 50 Gel 50g, pays cash; a confirmed, paid pharmacy invoice exists today.
Script: `scripts/ux/journeys/j06-walk-in-counter-sale.cjs`. Fresh-seed result (measured): `PASS j06 actions=15 decisions=2 reentry=2 screens=9`. Screenshots: `output/ux-journeys/j06-check/j06/`.

The pharmacist dashboard (`01-dashboard.png`) has no pharmacy action at all (only Users, Branches, System Alerts), so every route starts from the sidebar.

## 2. Take-away Pass

**Dashboard (`01-dashboard.png`)**: Zero pharmacy content for the pharmacist: Total Users, Active Users, Total Branches, System Status are admin numbers. The most frequent pharmacist task (a sale) is two sidebar clicks away but the home screen offers no "New sale" button. Remove the four stat cards for this role; put "New sale" there.

**Sales register (`02-sales-register.png`)**: One primary action ("New sale", dark, top left of the actions) is right. The register (filters, six past bills) is not needed to start a sale, but it is the page the sidebar item opens. Collapse: make the sidebar item open the blank bill and move the register behind a "Bills" tab.

**New bill (`03`, `04`, `05`, `06`)**:
- The Rx queue card (Meera Shah, three medicines, "Load") fills the first screen. For a walk-in it is pure noise and it pushes the form below the fold. Collapse it behind a "From prescription" button.
- "Patient & Doctor Details", "Add Items to Invoice", "Invoice Summary", "Billing Information" are four cards where the job has three things: items, customer (optional), pay. "Doctor" (optional) is shown for a no-prescription sale.
- "Individual Drugs / Treatment Packages" tabs and the "A- 100% A+" font controls (`TabsFontSizeControls`, `:2348`) are not needed to sell two tablets.
- "Bill discount" with `%` and `₹` toggles is shown at 0 on every sale. Defer.
- Per item: "Unit Price", "Discount %", "Tax %" are editable inputs plus a "Prescription directions" block (Dosage, Frequency, Duration, Instructions, `:2819`) on a non-prescription sale. Take away: the directions block for drugs added by hand, and tax as an input.
- Hierarchy: the search result's "Add" is the loud element but the whole row is already clickable (`:2392-2405`), so the button is a second way to do the same thing. Search clears itself after adding (`:1091`), good.
- Two primary-looking buttons: "Print Preview" (dark, full width) and "Save draft" (outline). One primary, good; but the label names a screen, not the result.
- Stated twice: patient name appears in "Patient" and again in "Billing Name"; total in "Invoice Summary" and under the button "2 items . ₹991.20 total".

**Print preview dialog (`07`, `08`)**: A full-screen dialog with Copy Type (Original/Duplicate), Preview Zoom, an invoice/status/patient/total card, "Close", "Confirm & Print", "Download PDF" (disabled until confirmed) and "Confirm Invoice". Two buttons confirm. Invoice number, status, patient and total are repeated in the header chips, the paper preview and the side card (three times).

## 3. The Simpler Version

1. Dashboard shows "New sale". One click opens a blank bill with the cursor in "Add medicine" (no Rx queue in the way; a "From prescription" button covers that case).
2. Type "Cetirizine", press Enter: the line is added, the cursor is in its Quantity. Type 10.
3. Type "Sunscreen", Enter (quantity 1 is the default).
4. Customer is optional and defaults to "Walk-in customer"; no patient, phone or doctor asked. Payment shows Cash selected.
5. Press one button "Confirm and print". The bill gets its number, stock moves, cash is recorded, the browser print dialog opens.
6. The page shows "Bill PHI-... paid, Rs 991.20" with "New sale" focused for the next customer.

That is 8 actions (menu, New sale, 2 typed searches, 2 Enters, 1 quantity, Confirm and print) against 15, and it removes the stranger-record hack.

## 7. Verdict

**Convoluted.** 15 actions against an honest 8, a forced stranger-record workaround that leaks that person's address onto a walk-in receipt, two buttons that both confirm, a checkout that ends on the filled form, and four names for one task. The core mechanics (medicine search that clears after adding, Cash as the default, stock deducted on confirm, goal reached: confirmed, paid bill with 10 and 1 units) work and are not the problem; the demands made for a customer the app does not need to know are. The cheapest win is the Enter-to-add change (2 actions, effort S). The most valuable is making the patient optional (5 actions, effort L).
